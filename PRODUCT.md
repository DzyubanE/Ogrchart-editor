# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small team leads and managers who need to communicate reporting structure without a dedicated ops or design background. They open the tool to build or update an org chart, then share a public read-only link with their team or leadership, or export it for embedding into internal docs (Confluence, Notion). Sessions are infrequent but purposeful: create/update a chart, check it looks right, share it, move on.

## Product Purpose

OrgChart Editor is a free, open-source visual editor for building organizational charts on an infinite drag-and-drop canvas. It autosaves to the cloud, generates shareable public links with no login required for viewers, and exports embeddable HTML. Success looks like: a manager can go from blank canvas to a shared, presentable chart in minutes, without fighting the tool.

## Brand Personality

Clean, fast, trustworthy. The product should feel like a straightforward productivity tool — minimal friction, gets the job done, and looks credible enough to share externally with leadership or across teams. Not flashy, not a toy.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS clichés: gradient-text heroes, cream/sand near-white backgrounds, hero-metric templates, tiny uppercase eyebrows, numbered section markers as default scaffolding, identical icon-grid feature cards. The current identity (deep indigo `#3730a3` accent, Geist typeface, restrained neutral surfaces) should be preserved and refined, not replaced with templated SaaS visual language.

## Design Principles

- **Trust over flash** — the tool is often shown to leadership; polish should read as credibility, not decoration.
- **Get out of the way** — the canvas and editing tools are the product; chrome should stay quiet so the chart is the focus.
- **One accent, used deliberately** — indigo carries brand identity; don't dilute it with competing saturated colors.
- **Consistent across surfaces** — dashboard, editor, and public view/export share the same visual language so a shared chart still feels like "OrgChart."
- **Practical for non-technical users** — favor plain language and obvious affordances over technical jargon or clever-but-unclear UI.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: sufficient color contrast (including on the indigo accent against light/dark surfaces), full keyboard navigation for editing and dialogs, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`. Charts may be viewed by non-technical, less tech-savvy audiences via public links, so legibility and clarity take priority over subtlety.
